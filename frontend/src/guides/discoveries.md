# Discoveries

What LoreStudio noticed in your story and has not added on its own: a new name, a line with no speaker, something an import could not place. Each waits here for your yes or no.

## Open it

Logo menu › **Discoveries**. The header says how many are waiting and when your prose was last looked through for names. **Look again** looks now. This page used to be called Proposals.

Nothing here changes your story until you say yes. The chips along the top, such as **People**, **Places** or **Dialogue**, narrow the list to one kind.

## What turns up here

- **Someone new** and **Somewhere new**: a name your prose uses that the Lorebook does not have, with how often it is named and where. **Add as a character** or **Add to Places** makes the entry. **Not a person** or **Not a place** keeps the name from asking again. The newest of them wait on the Overview too, under **What you've written**, with the same yes and no. A place that is named but not yet filled in also offers **Same as…**, to fold it into a place you already have, and **Not a place**.
- **Lines with no speaker**: lines of dialogue nobody can tell the speaker of, one row per scene. **Tag them** opens the scene's lines so you can say who speaks each; **Leave them** sets them aside.
- **From an import**: what an import brought that had no clear home. See below.
- **From the series**: in a book of a series, someone or somewhere the series shares who is named here but not yet in this book (**Add from the series**), or an entry of this book's with the same name as one of the series' (**The same one**).

In _The Last Lighthouse_, the prose calls the cottage `[[The Keeper's Cottage]]`, while Places has **Keeper's Cottage**. It waits here: **Same as…** › **Keeper's Cottage** makes them one place. **The Mainland**, the place Eleanor left and has not gone back to, waits too.

## Yes and no

- **Yes** adds it where it belongs (a character or a place in the Lorebook, a thread in Promises) and says where, with a link to open it. {{key:undo}} takes it back, like any edit.
- **No** keeps it from asking again. Lines with no speaker are the one exception: when the scene changes, they are a new question and come back.

A no is a change too, so logo menu › **Chronicle** › **Changes** can undo it.

## What an import couldn't place

When you import from another app, anything with no clear home waits under **From an import**, one group per import. Each item offers **Keep in Compendium**, or **Make into…** a character, a place, a thread, a trope, a note or anything else; **Don't need it** lets it go.

The group's header links to **The import's report**. **Keep the rest in the Compendium** and **Don't need any of these** each settle everything left at once, as one change, so one undo puts it all back. See [Import and export](/guides/import-and-export).

## Discoveries and Findings

Discoveries offers something new to add. **Findings** asks you to look again at something already in your story. See [Findings: what needs your eye](/guides/findings).

## In Studio: what the Assistant suggests

**Look again** also asks the Assistant, and its suggestions join the list marked **Assistant**:

- **Who is in a scene**: someone it reads as present in a scene. **Yes, they're here** records it.
- **What the reader learns**: a fact the reader learns, or a clue or misdirection. **The reader knows this** or **Add to what the reader knows** adds it to Promises › **What the reader knows**.
- **Between characters**: a relationship it suggests. **Add the relationship** keeps it on the characters' **Relationships**.
- New characters and places it finds in a scene. With discovery switched on, the Scene sheet's **Assistant** page has **Analyze for discoveries**.

The page's ⋯ › **What the Assistant looks for…** sets how it looks for new things in your prose: **Enable discovery features**, **Auto-analyze on save** (it reads each scene again after you save), what to **Look for** (characters, places, relationships, themes, objects) and a **Minimum confidence**. These change only what the Assistant does; the names found on your own machine always come in.
