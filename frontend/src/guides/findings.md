# Findings: what needs your eye

One list of what the checks noticed in your story and think you may want to look at. Each finding is a question, not a rule: you decide what it means.

## Open it

Logo menu › **Findings**. The header says how many there are and when the checks last ran.

You also meet findings where you work. **This scene** in the side panel has a **Findings** line when the scene has any, and the Scene sheet lists them under **Needs your eye**. A character's, place's, thread's or twist's sheet shows its own in a **Needs your eye** card, and shows nothing when there is nothing.

## What the checks look for

Every check here runs on your own machine. Most are always current:

- **Names**: a name close to one in the Lorebook. Type "Elenor" in a scene and it asks whether that is a slip for Eleanor Vance, or someone new.
- **Speakers**: someone who speaks but is not in the Lorebook.
- **The cast**: a main character who has not been on the page for the last five scenes.
- **Threads and twists**: a thread that goes quiet, closes before it opens, or opens inside another and closes after it; an open thread that runs through three scenes with no try that fails; a clue after its reveal, a reveal with no clue before it, several twists revealed at once; a trope you mean to turn whose thread never turns.
- **Chapters and length**: an empty chapter between written ones, or one with no words and no plan; prose sitting on a chapter or an act, outside any scene; a story nearing or past the length its form usually runs to.
- **Series**: books that disagree about something meant to stay true across them.

_The Last Lighthouse_'s Chapter 7: The Mainland has no words yet, but it has a plan and comes last. It is just ahead, so nothing is raised.

The rest run when you ask: **Run checks** › **Prose habits, tense, point of view** looks for passive voice, adverbs, dialogue tags other than said, words repeated close together, sentences all of one length, and slips of tense or point of view. In a script it reads the action lines. It runs as a job, so you can keep writing.

## Views

**By place** groups findings by scene in reading order, then by character, place, thread or twist, then the whole story. **By urgency** sorts them into **Look at these first**, **Worth a look** and **Small things**. The chips along the top, such as **Prose** or **Cast**, narrow the list to one kind.

## Act on a finding

- The button on the row does the one thing it suggests: **Open the scene**, **Show in the scene** (the words light up), **Open the sheet**, or a fix such as **Change to “Eleanor”** or **Add to the Lorebook**. A fix says exactly what it will change and waits for your yes; **Not now** leaves it.
- **It's intended**, in the row's ⋯ menu, dismisses it. A finding about a scene comes back if that scene changes. The foot of the page counts how many you have dismissed.
- Fixes and dismissals are changes like any other: {{key:undo}} takes one back, and so does **Undo** beside it in logo menu › **Chronicle** › **Changes**.

## Findings and Discoveries

Findings asks you to look again at something already in your story. **Discoveries** offers something new to add, such as a name the Lorebook does not have yet, and waits for a yes or a no. See [Discoveries](/guides/discoveries).

## In Studio: the Assistant's checks

**Run checks** also lists the Assistant's checks, run only when you ask: **Pacing**, **Plot holes**, **Continuity**, **Character depth**, **Themes**, **Clichés**, **First-pass editor**, **Story compass**, **Story economy** and the **Editorial pass…**. Their findings join the list marked **Assistant**. On any row, ⋯ › **Ask about this** opens a conversation about it, and **Read the whole run** opens the full answer in the Chronicle.
