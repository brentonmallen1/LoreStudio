# Dialogue and quotes

Keep track of who says what, without changing how you write.

## Tag who is speaking

A speaker tag goes straight after the closing quote: `"I know."<Calder>`. Any quote marks work, and the name can be any name on the character's sheet. Tags never appear in an export.

- Type `^` and pick the speaker: an empty line in quote marks appears.
- Select spoken words and press {{key:attributeDialogue}}, or choose **Attribute**.
- Type `<` after a closing quote: the likeliest speaker comes first.

> **Tip:** You need not tag every line. A speech tag like `"…," Maya said` or two speakers taking turns is usually enough.

## See the conversation

Choose **Dialogue** on the side panel's rail to see the scene's lines as a conversation.

- A line with no speaker sits in the middle. Choose **Who says this?** to tag it.
- An italic thought reads **A thought**; it cannot be tagged.
- **Tag the dialogue** finds every untagged quote and suggests a speaker.

In Studio mode, **Suggest speakers** asks the Assistant who says each untagged line.

To see it in the prose, turn on the scene's ⋯ menu › **Type and width** › **Highlight dialogue**. Lines with no speaker, and tags that name nobody, show in amber; point at a tag to fix it.

## Make the quotes match

Mixed straight and curly quotes are a common copy-edit note. On the Scene sheet's **Findings** page, **Quote style** counts both and makes them all one kind, telling you first how many will change. Speaker tags are never touched.

The **Dialogue** section of [Numbers](/guides/numbers) shows each speaker's share of the words, and the scenes where one voice holds the floor.
