# Getting started

A short tour: what LoreStudio is for, a first session, and how to find your way around.

## What LoreStudio is for

LoreStudio is a writing app that helps you plan, organize and understand your story. You write every word. LoreStudio keeps track of who is in it, what you have promised the reader and where it is going, so your mind can stay on the page.

It has two modes: **Writer** keeps to your story and the tools around it, and **Studio** adds more. A new account is asked which it wants the first time it signs in. Switch at the bottom of the logo menu, or in **Settings › Mode**.

Studio adds the **Assistant** and the other AI tools. They answer questions and point things out; they never write your prose. Set up a model in **Settings › AI / LLM**.

## Your first session

1. Choose **New story** on your stories page (logo menu › **All stories**). Say what it's called and what you are writing: a novel or story, a screenplay or a stage play.
2. Under **How do you like to work?**, choose one. There is no right answer, and every tool stays available whichever you choose:
   - **I like to plan first** opens the **Outline**, where you jot down what happens, a line for each scene, before you write.
   - **I find the story as I write** opens the first scene on a blank page. As you write, the Overview's **What you've written** keeps your scenes in a line each, who and where your prose names, and offers to tidy up when you're ready.
   - **A bit of both, or not sure yet** opens the story's **Overview**.

   New to writing a long story? **First story?**, under the three, offers the **First story guide**: seven short steps from an idea in a sentence to your first scene. It stays in the logo menu with how far you have got, so you can come back to any step. **More options** holds a shape for the story's big parts (**Freeform** is fine), and whether it is part of a series. You can change how you like to work later in **Lorebook › Story identity**.

3. Write. The light at the bottom right turns green a moment after you pause: your words are saved. Type `/` in the text for its commands: `/dialogue` for a line someone says, `/note` and `/todo` for the margin, `/image`, `/character` and `/place` to name someone or somewhere from the Lorebook.
4. Open **This scene** in the side panel to see the scene at a glance. Choose **Scene sheet** there to fill in what happens, who is there and why the scene matters.
5. Add your next chapter or scene from the strip: press {{key:cycleStrip}} to widen it, then use **+**.

Already have a draft? **Import** on your stories page brings it in. See [Import and export](/guides/import-and-export).

> **Tip:** The samples, under [Samples](#samples) below, are stories with every part of LoreStudio filled in. Load them and open _Sample: Novel · The Last Lighthouse_ to see what each page looks like in use.

## Finding your way around

- **Logo menu** (top left): every page of the story and its sections, then **Guides**, **Settings**, light and dark, the mode, your last backup of everything and your account.
- **Command palette** ({{key:palette}}): type a few letters to reach any page or action.
- **Breadcrumbs** (along the top): click a crumb to open that page; the › after it lists what is inside.
- **The strip** (down the left): your book's scenes as stops on a line, so you always know where you are. {{key:cycleStrip}} widens it to name every act, chapter and scene. **Key**, under its color picker, says what its colors and shapes mean.
- **Side panel** (on the right): a rail of buttons for **This scene**, **Characters**, **Places**, **Threads**, **Notes**, **Freewrite** and **Dialogue**. Choose the one already showing to fold the panel away. {{key:togglePanel}} shows or hides it. The first time, each button wears its name; point at one, or reach it with Tab, to see it again, or choose **Show what the side panel's buttons do** in the palette.
- **Tabs** in the side panel: whatever you opened beside your prose, such as a character or a place. Choose **Open beside the prose** on any page's ⋯ menu to keep that page in a tab while you write.
- **Status corner** (bottom right): the save light (green saved, yellow saving, red offline or a conflict) and the scene's word count. Click it for the chapter, the book, today and your goal.
- **The scene's ⋯ menu**: type and width, notes in the margin, a writing sprint, images and the scene's tools.

<!-- studio -->

In Studio mode the rail also has the **Assistant**. On a sheet in the Lorebook or Promises, **Assistant** in the header lists what it can do for that character, place or thread, each with a line on what it does. Its answers stay in a band under the header, one per question, until you clear them or ask again: **Talk it over** opens one in the Assistant, and **Fold away** tucks the band out of sight. Everywhere else the Assistant answers once (a summary on Story identity, a recap on the Overview, guidance on a Plan step, a reading of an image), the answer stays where you asked for it in the same way, even after you quit and come back, with **Talk it over**, **Ask again** and **Clear** beside it.
<!-- /studio -->

A few keys worth learning early:

- {{key:back}} and {{key:forward}} go back and forward through where you have been, as in a browser.
- {{key:prevScene}} and {{key:nextScene}} step through the book's scenes in order.
- {{key:focusMode}} hides everything but the page.
- {{key:help}} lists every shortcut.

## Make it comfortable

**Settings › Typography** sets the **Interface size**, the **Writing size** and the **Line width**; the scene's ⋯ menu › **Type and width** changes the last two without leaving the page. Your settings follow your account to every device you sign in from.

LoreStudio also runs as an app on Macs with Apple silicon. **Settings › About and updates** shows your version and checks for a new one.

## The tutorial

Two stories teach LoreStudio by doing: **Learn LoreStudio** for prose and **Learn Screenplays** for scripts. Each scene is a lesson, with a card above the text saying what to try and ticking off what you do. A new account starts with both; delete them when you are done. Make a copy here at any time; one you already have stays, with whatever you wrote in it.
