# Connections

A map of what your story's people, places and scenes are to each other, drawn from what you have already written. Use it to see who is tied to what, and what stands alone.

## Open it

Logo menu › **Lorebook** › **Connections**. The first time, choose **Build the graph**. It takes a moment and runs as a job on your own machine, so you can keep writing.

Nothing on the map is invented. Every dot stands for something in your story, and every line for a link you made or one that follows from your prose. Building it reads your story and nothing else, so Writer mode has it too.

## What the dots are

Each dot is one thing, colored by kind:

- **Character**, **Place** and **Scene**.
- **Thread**, **Twist** and **Trope**.
- **Fact**: something the reader learns, from **What the reader knows** and your twists' reveals.

## What the lines are

A line says how two things are linked. Point at one to read it:

- From your sheets: **related to** (a relationship between two characters), **point of view**, **set in** (a scene's place), **advances** (a scene a thread runs through), **revealed in** and **hinted at in** (a twist's reveal and its clues), **links to** (a setup and its payoff), and a trope's **plays in**, **carried by** and **tracked by**.
- From your prose: **followed by** (reading order), **speaks in** (a character with a line in the scene), and **present in** (someone the scene shows, from its point of view, a line they speak, or their name in the prose).
- From what the reader knows: **established in** (where a fact comes out) and **knows** (who learns it: anyone listed, or anyone in the scene when it comes out). A mention alone does not count: being named in a scene is not being in it.

In _The Last Lighthouse_, the twist _The Visitor Has Been Here Before_ is hinted at in Knock at the Door, The Logbook and The Gap, and revealed in What Thomas Knew. Focus on it, and those four scenes gather around it.

## Find your way around it

- **Focus on** picks one thing, and **How far** shows what is **1 step away**, **2 steps away** or **3 steps away** from it. Focus on a minor character to see how little holds them in the story.
- The buttons along the top hide or show one kind, such as every scene.
- Click a dot to open its panel: what it is, a line about it, and everything it connects to, grouped by kind of link. Click any of those to move to it.

## Keep it current

The map is built when you ask, not as you type. After you write or change your sheets, choose **Rebuild graph** at the top of the page. The header counts the things and connections in it.

## In Studio: what the Assistant adds

The map is part of what the Assistant knows about your story. Studio adds two things to it:

- **Dashed lines** are links the Assistant suggested and you have not answered yet, such as someone it reads as present in a scene. They wait in **Discoveries** for your yes or no.
- A dot's panel also says how many of its passages are indexed for the Assistant to search, and how many calls to the Assistant were about it.

Settings › **Codex**, the Assistant's index of your stories, builds the map and the search index for any story, and sets the model the index uses.
