# The Lorebook

Everything that is true in your story: what it is and why you are writing it, its people, its places and how its world works. Keep it here once, and every page that needs it reads it from here.

## Its sections

Open logo menu › **Lorebook** and pick a section:

- **Story Identity**: what the story is, in five groups (**Foundation**, **Voice**, **Form**, **Substance** and **Compass**), from the logline and premise to the point of view, the structure, the themes, the tropes in play and your goals. A rail beside it follows your place; in a narrow window, use **Jump to**.
- **Characters**: who is in the story. A character's sheet has **Overview**, **Who are they**, **Dialogue**, **Arc journey** and **Relationships**. See [Who are they: writing people](/guides/who-are-they).
- **Places**: where it happens. A place can sit inside another: in _The Last Lighthouse_, Harrow Island holds The Lighthouse, Keeper's Cottage, The Shoals and The Village. **Add a place inside**, on a place's sheet, makes one there.
- **Systems**: the rules the world runs on, such as its technology, its faith or its trade. _The Light_ is one: it must be on by dusk and must not go out before dawn.
- **Cultures**: the peoples of the world, what they value and what they never do. The Islanders leave food on doorsteps during illness, and no one knocks.
- **History**: eras, and the events in each. _The Keeper's Era_ holds _The Great Storm_ and _Thomas Vance Becomes Keeper_.
- **Calendars**: how the people of your story count their days: months, days of the week and special days.
- **Travel**: routes between two places, how long they take and how. From Harrow Island to The Lighthouse is 20 minutes on foot, along the cliff edge.
- **Connections**: a map of how the story's people, places and scenes connect, built from what you have written. See [Connections](/guides/connections).
- **Series**: what this book shares with the other books of its series. See [Writing a series](/guides/writing-a-series).

Threads, twists and what the reader knows have a group of their own, logo menu › **Promises**. See [Threads, twists and what the reader knows](/guides/promises).

## Sheets

Characters, Places, Systems, Cultures, History, Calendars and Travel are each a list with a sheet beside it. **Add** at the top of the list (on History, **Era** or **Event**) makes a new entry, named and ready to type over; **Find in…** narrows the list.

Click the name at the top of a sheet to rename it; a route takes its name from the two places it joins. A character's or a place's sheet has more:

- The dot beside the name sets its color.
- Under the name are the scenes it appears in: click one to open it.
- ⋯ › **Add another name…** keeps a name the prose also uses. Thomas Vance is also called Tom, so a mention of Tom is a mention of him.
- **Open beside the page** keeps the sheet in a tab of the side panel while you write.
- When the checks noticed something about it, a **Needs your eye** card says what. See [Findings: what needs your eye](/guides/findings).

## Mentions

Type `@` and a name in a scene to mention a character, or `[[` to mention a place: _"@Eleanor Vance stood at the window and watched [[The Lighthouse]] beam sweep through the dark."_ A list opens as you type; **Tab** or **Enter** takes one.

A mention links your words to the Lorebook, so the scene knows who and where it is about, and the sheet lists the scene. A name the Lorebook does not have yet waits in **Discoveries** for a yes or a no. See [Discoveries](/guides/discoveries).

## In Studio: the Assistant on a sheet

A sheet's header has **Assistant**, a menu of what it can do for that entry, each with a line on what it does. A character's offers **Interview**, a conversation with them, and **Suggest attributes**, as well as **Character depth**, **Arc Analysis** and **Voice Fidelity**; a place's, **Brainstorm what exists** and **Suggest creatures, plants and names**.

Its answer stays in a band under the header, with when it was asked, until you clear it or ask again: **Talk it over** takes it to the Assistant to discuss, **Ask again** asks once more, **Clear** removes it and **Fold away** tucks the band out of sight. In the sample, The Lighthouse's sheet already keeps an answer to **Brainstorm what exists**.
