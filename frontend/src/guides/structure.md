# Structure and templates

How your story is built from parts, and how to add, move and plan them.

## Your story is an outline

Every story is an outline of parts inside parts: scenes inside chapters, chapters inside acts. The story's **shape** (its structure template) names those levels. You pick one under **More options › A shape to start from** when you choose **New story**; **Freeform** is fine, and you can add parts later.

- **Three acts**: acts, chapters and scenes.
- **Freeform**: sections and scenes, for a story that doesn't fit a shape yet.
- Others follow a known pattern, such as **The hero's journey**.

Tick **Start with an outline** to begin with the shape's parts already in place and a first scene ready to write in. **Make your own shape**, under the list, lets you make one with whatever levels you need.

## The Outline

The **Outline** (logo menu › **Outline**) is what happens in your story, part by part: your acts, chapters and scenes as lines, each with its title and a line of what happens. Plan there before you write, or watch it fill in as you write.

- **Type a line** to change it: its title, then **what happens** after it. **→** at the end of a title goes on to what happens.
- **Enter** adds the next line. A line with nothing typed in it goes away again.
- **Tab** tucks a line under the one above it, a level deeper. **Shift+Tab** moves it out a level: a scene becomes a chapter that holds the scenes after it. A scene with words always stays a scene, because your prose lives in scenes.
- **↑** and **↓** move between lines. **Esc** leaves the text, so **Tab** reaches the line's buttons; **Enter** goes back to typing.
- **Drag** a line by its handle (⋮⋮) to move it among lines of its own level, or into another chapter.
- Click the arrow at a scene's end to unfold it: **Why it's here**, **Whose eyes**, its status and words, **Write this scene** and its **Scene sheet**.
- A scene with no words yet is **planned**, and its mark is dashed.
- A scene with words and no line of what happens shows its first sentence instead, marked **from the first line**, until you write a line of your own. A line too long for its row ends in **…**: point at it, or reach the row with the keyboard, to read it all, or unfold the scene.

Two ways to look at it, top right: **List** and **Cards**, a column per chapter and a card per scene, to drag about as on a corkboard. Each comes in two orders: **As the reader meets it**, and **As it happens**, the order of the story's own time (place scenes on it from the List). Your choice is remembered on this computer.

Beside the prose, **Open beside the prose** under the page's ⋯ shows the same lines narrow, with the scene you are writing marked; click a line to go there. The strip on the writing page still widens with {{key:cycleStrip}}.

## Not placed yet

Beside the Outline sits the **Not placed yet** tray: ideas for things that should happen, not tied to a scene. On a narrow window it folds above the Outline; beside the prose it sits under the lines.

- **Jot an idea…** at its foot: type, and **Enter** keeps it.
- **Drag** an idea into the List or the Cards when you know where it goes: above or below a scene, or onto a chapter to go at its end. It becomes a planned scene there and leaves the tray. A short idea becomes the scene's title; a longer one keeps all its words as what happens, under a title made of its first words. Its notes go with it, as a note on the scene.
- **Place it…** does the same from the keyboard: choose the chapter and the spot from a list.
- An idea's **⋯** renames, moves it up or down the tray, or deletes it. Drag ideas up and down the tray, too.

## What you've written

If you find the story as you write, the Overview keeps up with you under **What you've written** (for a story you plan, it is a section to open at the foot of the Overview, or **What you've written** in the palette). Everything in it is read from your prose on this computer, and nothing changes until you say so.

- **Your story so far**: each scene in a line, from its line of what happens or else its first line, marked **from the first line**. Type in a line to say it better; it becomes the scene's line in the Outline too.
- **People you've written about** and **Places you've written about**: who and where in your Lorebook the prose names, and how often. A name the prose uses that the Lorebook doesn't have waits there with **Add as a person** and **Not a person** (or the same for a place); the rest wait in Discoveries. **Look for new names** reads the prose for them now.
- **When you're ready to tidy up**: offers to organize what you've written, each with **Not now**, which the story remembers. A scene of **2,500 words or more** where a paragraph opens on a jump in time ("By morning", "The next day", "Three days on", "Later that night", "Weeks passed" and the like) is offered a split there. The rule is a rule of thumb: most scenes run 1,000 to 2,500 words, and a longer one that jumps in time is often two. **Show me the places** opens the scene with each place lit and the cursor at the first, ready for **Split the scene here**. The same offer shows in the side panel's **This scene**. A chapter that holds **8 scenes or more** is offered **Make chapters**, which opens the Outline to choose some of its scenes and wrap them; that is a rule of thumb too, and many fine chapters hold more.

## Organize what you've written

Scenes found as you write rarely arrive in their final shape. Each of these is one change, and {{key:undo}} takes it back whole.

- **Split the scene here**: put the cursor where the new scene should start, then choose it from the scene's ⋯ menu, type `/split`, or find it in the palette. Name the new scene (it starts as the scene's title with "(continued)"); it takes the words after the cursor and sits right after this one, in the same chapter. Notes in the margin and lines of dialogue go with their words.
- **Join with the next scene**: from the scene's ⋯ menu, the palette, or an unfolded scene in the Outline. The next scene's words follow this one's after a scene break (a rule across the page, the same break an import reads as a new scene), so the seam stays where it was; delete the break if you want none. Their lines of what happens join, the joined scene starts where the first started and ends where the second ended, and notes, links, threads, places and who is there move over. Scenes join only within one chapter: move one first to join across.
- **Wrap in a chapter**: in the Outline's List, **Shift+click** scenes, or press **Shift+↓** and **Shift+↑** on a line, to choose scenes that sit next to each other; **Esc** or a plain click lets them go. **Wrap in a chapter** (in your shape's own word: a section, a sequence…) asks for a name and puts them in a new chapter in their place. Scenes from the middle of a chapter leave its later scenes in a chapter of their own after it, named "(continued)", so the order never changes. A shape whose parts sit side by side, such as Single MICE, has no level to wrap scenes in.

## Takes: another version of a scene

Not sure a scene works? Write it another way without losing the first. **Try another version**, from the scene's ⋯ menu or the palette, copies the whole scene into a new **take** and opens it; change as much or as little as you like. The scene's header then shows its takes, **Take 1 · first draft**, **Take 2** and so on, with **+ Try another version** for one more. A click on a take opens it to write; what you type saves to the take that is open.

- **The take that counts** wears ✓. It is the scene everywhere else: your manuscript, exports, word counts, the strip, checks and findings, and search. The others are kept until you delete them.
- **Use this take** (in the header while a take that does not count is open, or in **Compare**) makes the open take the one that counts. Its words become the scene's; the take that counted keeps its words as before.
- **Compare** shows two takes side by side, one above the other in the side panel, with the words only take 1 has struck through on red and the words only take 2 has on green. Unmarked text is the same in both. Rework one passage and only that passage is marked. With three takes or more, choose which two.
- **The scene's ⋯ menu** renames the open take ("Calder speaks first") or deletes it, and has **Compare takes** too. The take that counts is deleted only once another counts; down to one take, the header is quiet again.
- **This scene** in the side panel and the scene's line in the Outline say "2 takes" when there is more than one, and This scene's **Compare** shows them there.

Making, switching, choosing, renaming and deleting takes are each one change {{key:undo}} takes back. Typing in a take is undone as typing always is, take by take: switching to another starts its own history, so {{key:undo}} never carries one take's typing into another. A scene with several takes is not split or joined: keep the one that counts and delete the others first.

## Classic patterns

A **classic pattern** is a list of moments many stories share, and roughly where each one falls: **Three turning points**, **Save the Cat**, **The story circle** and **The hero's journey**, plus any you make yourself. Turning one on changes nothing in your story; it shows on your Outline, so you can see which moments you have and which you might want.

- **Pattern**, at the top of the Outline, chooses one, or **None**. It's the same choice as **Classic pattern** on Story identity and as following a pattern in Plan with questions.
- A scene that carries a moment wears a small tag with the moment's name. Click it for what the moment is, in plain words, and **Not this one** to take it off.
- **Where your story stands**, above the Outline, puts each moment where it tends to fall: filled when a scene carries it, dashed when nothing does yet, over a bar of how much of the story has words (the share of its scenes with words in them). Click a moment for its card: what it is, roughly where it falls, and **In your story**, the scene that carries it, with **Open in the Outline** and **Not this one**. **Give it to a scene…** chooses one.
- Unfold a scene in the List to choose the moment it carries, under **Carries the moment**.

Moments with nothing there yet aren't mistakes. A pattern is a map of what often works, not a rule.

## Acts

Narrowed, the strip marks each act with its numeral (**I**, **II**, **III**) and each chapter with its number, counted through the whole book. The Outline shows the same numbers beside each act and chapter. Hover over a numeral to see the act's title and how long it is; click it to open the act's page.

## Prose outside a scene

Your words belong in scenes: the strip, the export and Numbers count only scenes. A draft imported before LoreStudio did this for you can have words on a chapter itself. **Findings** lists them, and the chapter's page says how many words it holds. **Give them a scene** moves them into a new first scene named for the chapter, and {{key:undo}} takes it back.

## Plan a chapter

Open an act or a chapter, or any part with parts inside it, to see its page. It says where the part falls in the book, such as "Chapter 4 of 13". Write a **Synopsis** (what happens across it) and **Why it's here** (what it does for the story that nothing else does). Its scenes sit below as cards; add one there.

## Plan a scene

Open the scene's **Scene sheet**: choose **Scene sheet** in the side panel's **This scene**, or **Open the scene sheet** in the scene's ⋯ menu.

- **What happens**: the scene in a sentence or two.
- **Starts** and **Ends**: where your character or the situation stands as the scene starts and as it ends. A scene where nothing changes is worth a second look.
- **Why it's here** (under **The plan**): what the scene does that nothing else does. It is the most useful field when you revise.
- **Key events**: the moments that must land.
- **Who**, **Where**, **Beat** and **When**: click them to change who is in the scene, where and when it happens, and which moment of a classic pattern it carries.

## Scene status

Every scene is **Planned**, **Draft**, **Revised** or **Final**. Change it from the status beside the scene's title. A planned scene shows in italics in the strip, and becomes a draft as soon as you write in it. When you export, you can include only the scenes at a given status.
