# Structure and templates

How your story is built from parts, and how to add, move and plan them.

## Your story is an outline

Every story is an outline of parts inside parts: scenes inside chapters, chapters inside acts. The story's **shape** (its structure template) names those levels. You pick one under **More options › A shape to start from** when you choose **New story**; **Freeform** is fine, and you can add parts later.

- **Three acts**: acts, chapters and scenes.
- **Freeform**: sections and scenes, for a story that doesn't fit a shape yet.
- Others follow a known pattern, such as **The hero's journey**.

Tick **Start with an outline** to begin with the shape's parts already in place and a first scene ready to write in. **Make your own shape**, under the list, lets you make one with whatever levels you need.

## The Outline

The **Outline** (logo menu › **Outline**) is what happens in your story, part by part: your acts, chapters and scenes as lines, each with its title and a line of what happens. Plan there before you write, or watch it fill in as you write.

- **Type a line** to change it: its title, then **what happens** after it. **→** at the end of a title goes on to what happens.
- **Enter** adds the next line. A line with nothing typed in it goes away again.
- **Tab** tucks a line under the one above it, a level deeper. **Shift+Tab** moves it out a level: a scene becomes a chapter that holds the scenes after it. A scene with words always stays a scene, because your prose lives in scenes.
- **↑** and **↓** move between lines. **Esc** leaves the text, so **Tab** reaches the line's buttons; **Enter** goes back to typing.
- **Drag** a line by its handle (⋮⋮) to move it among lines of its own level, or into another chapter.
- Click the arrow at a scene's end to unfold it: **Why it's here**, **Whose eyes**, its status and words, **Write this scene** and its **Scene sheet**.
- A scene with no words yet is **planned**, and its mark is dashed.

Two ways to look at it, top right: **List** and **Cards**, a column per chapter and a card per scene, to drag about as on a corkboard. Each comes in two orders: **As the reader meets it**, and **As it happens**, the order of the story's own time (place scenes on it from the List). Your choice is remembered on this computer.

Beside the prose, **Open beside the prose** under the page's ⋯ shows the same lines narrow, with the scene you are writing marked; click a line to go there. The strip on the writing page still widens with {{key:cycleStrip}}.

Every change can be undone with {{key:undo}}.

## Acts

Narrowed, the strip marks each act with its numeral (**I**, **II**, **III**) and each chapter with its number, counted through the whole book. The Outline shows the same numbers beside each act and chapter. Hover over a numeral to see the act's title and how long it is; click it to open the act's page.

## Prose outside a scene

Your words belong in scenes: the strip, the export and Numbers count only scenes. A draft imported before LoreStudio did this for you can have words on a chapter itself. **Findings** lists them, and the chapter's page says how many words it holds. **Give them a scene** moves them into a new first scene named for the chapter, and {{key:undo}} takes it back.

## Plan a chapter

Open an act or a chapter, or any part with parts inside it, to see its page. It says where the part falls in the book, such as "Chapter 4 of 13". Write a **Synopsis** (what happens across it) and **Why it's here** (what it does for the story that nothing else does). Its scenes sit below as cards; add one there.

## Plan a scene

Open the scene's **Scene sheet**: choose **Scene sheet** in the side panel's **This scene**, or **Open the scene sheet** in the scene's ⋯ menu.

- **What happens**: the scene in a sentence or two.
- **Starts** and **Ends**: where your character or the situation stands as the scene starts and as it ends. A scene where nothing changes is worth a second look.
- **Why it's here** (under **The plan**): what the scene does that nothing else does. It is the most useful field when you revise.
- **Key events**: the moments that must land.
- **Who**, **Where**, **Beat** and **When**: click them to change who is in the scene, where and when it happens, and which beat of the plan it serves.

## Scene status

Every scene is **Planned**, **Draft**, **Revised** or **Final**. Change it from the status beside the scene's title. A planned scene shows in italics in the strip, and becomes a draft as soon as you write in it. When you export, you can include only the scenes at a given status.
