# Writing a series

Start a series, plan it, share its people between books, and keep its promises from one book to the next.

## Start a series

Plan a series from the start, or let one grow from a book you have finished.

- **New series** on the dashboard makes a series before any of its books. Start blank, or from a **shape** such as a trilogy or a generational saga; each says what it adds before it adds it.
- In **New story**, tick **The first book of a series**.
- **Write a sequel** on a story's card starts the book after it. If that book was not in a series, the two become one.
- For a book you already have: logo menu › **Lorebook** › **Series**, then **Add it to** a series or **Start the series**.

## Start the next book

1. Choose **Write a sequel**, **New book** on the series page, or **New book in this series** in the palette ({{key:palette}}).
2. Name the book, then choose **Next: who carries over**.
3. Tick the characters, places and parts of the world the new book needs. Each starts where the last book left it.
4. Keep the **Plot threads still open** and **Twists not yet revealed** ticked, unless the new book leaves them alone. Unanswered questions are what a sequel most easily drops.

Genre, tone, point of view and byline carry over too.

## Find your way around

The series has its own page. Open it from the dashboard, or from the first crumb at the top of any of its books; the › beside it lists the books.

- **Overview**: the books in reading order, and **Needs your eye**. The arrows beside a book move it earlier or later.
- **Plan**: every book side by side.
- **Canon**: every character, place and part of the world the books share.
- **Promises**: every thread and twist across the books.
- **The story so far**: what each book leaves the reader with, a reminder before you write the next.
- **Shared research**: research, images and diagrams every book has.

## Plan the series

The **Plan** is a board with a column per book. Nothing in it is required, and none of it stops you writing.

- **Premise** and **Intent**: what the books are about together, and why it takes more than one.
- **Part**: what each book does that the others don't.
- **Arc**: the series' turning points. **Edit the beats** places each on the books that carry it.
- **Add a planned book** makes a book with no words yet, with its own Plan and Lorebook. **Bring in the cast** fills it from the book before.

New to this? **Walk me through it** goes a step at a time, with an example.

## Plan what changes from book to book

Some series turn on one thing: a new viewpoint, era or place for each book. Under the board, **Add a row**: **Viewpoint**, **Era**, **Place** or **Something else**.

1. In each book's cell, choose a character, era or place, or write an idea in words.
2. On a viewpoint row, tick **Seen through their eyes** to have each book checked against it.

The checks ask, never decide. A prologue in another voice may be meant to be; dismiss it.

> **Tip:** In a book, Numbers › **Whose eyes** shows the viewpoint rotating scene by scene.

## Share characters and places

What the books share is the series' **Canon**. Add to it from a sheet's ⋯ menu › **Share with the series**, or by carrying something over into a new book.

In a book, each Lorebook list ends with **From the series**: what this book doesn't have yet. **Add** brings one in where the book before left it. The @ picker offers them too.

## See how someone changes

Some things about a character stay true in every book, like where she was born. Others are meant to change, like what she wants. Open her in the Canon, or the **How … changes** fold on her sheet, to see both side by side.

If a field works differently in your series, choose **Let it change book to book** or **Keep it the same in every book** beside it.

## Keep promises across books

A thread asked in Book 1 can be answered in Book 3. Its sheet says **Carried from Book 1** or **Continues in Book 2**, and **Across the series** shows what each book does with it.

- A twist can be clued in one book and revealed in another.
- A setup can pay off in another book: link the two scenes on Promises › **Setups and payoffs**.
- In a later book, **What the reader knows** starts with **Coming in**: what the reader brings from the books before.

## Check the books agree

When two books disagree about something that should stay true, it shows in each book's **Findings** and in the series' **Needs your eye**. So does a thread left open that no later book picks up.

- **Use this in every book** makes one book's version everyone's.
- **Bring into Book 2** carries an open thread on.
- A deliberate change is fine: dismiss it, and it is dismissed in every book.

> **Tip:** Undo works book by book. Changes to the series itself (its name, order, arc and rows) cannot be undone.

## What the Assistant knows

The **Assistant** working on a book knows what its people and places were like in the books before it, never after. It also knows the threads they left open and what the reader comes in knowing.
