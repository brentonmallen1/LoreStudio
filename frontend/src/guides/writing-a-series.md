# Writing a series

Most series aren't planned as series. You finish a book and find the story isn't done with its people. LoreStudio works the same way: any book can become the first of a series the moment you start the next one.

## Starting the next book

On the dashboard, a story card's **Write a sequel** opens a new book that follows it. So does **New story**, if you choose the book it's a sequel to under **A sequel to**. The new book takes the old one's structure, genre, tone, point of view, byline and themes; change any of them later on its Story identity.

The next step asks **who carries over**: every character, place and part of the world of the book before, by kind. Tick the ones the new book needs. Each starts the new book as it stood at the end of the old one, and goes on changing there. Relationships come too when both people do, a place keeps the place it is inside when that comes too, and a character's portrait comes with her (a copy of her own, so her face can change in the new book).

The same step offers the **threads still open** and the **twists not yet revealed**, already ticked: the questions the books have asked and not answered are the ones a sequel most easily drops. Untick any the new book leaves alone.

If the first book wasn't in a series yet, it is now: the series is named after it, and you can rename it.

## The series and its books

A series has its own page. Open it from the dashboard, where its books sit together in order, or from the series name in the bar at the top of any of its books. That bar's menu moves between the books or starts the next one.

The page has sections. The **Overview** holds what the series is (its name, its premise, what it's for), its books in reading order, and what needs your eye across them. The **Canon** is every character, place and part of the world the books share. **Promises**, **The story so far** and **Shared research** are below. Move a book earlier or later and the order changes what each book starts from. Take a book out of the series and it keeps everything in it, now its own.

## What stays true, and what changes

Some things about a character stay true across every book: where she was born, what she looks like. Others are meant to change: what she wants, who she is now. LoreStudio calls the first kind **enduring** and the second **evolving**.

Open anything in the Canon, or **How she changes** on its sheet, to see both. What stays true is said once while the books agree. What changes is shown book by book, so you can see her arc across the series in one place, with books that say the same thing folded together.

If the defaults are wrong for your series (appearance does change across your books), point at the field and choose **Let it change book to book**, or **Keep it the same in every book**. It applies to every element of that kind in the series.

## When books disagree

When two books disagree about something that should stay true, it's a finding, in each book and on the series page under **Needs your eye**. It's a question, not a verdict: which one is it? **Use this in every book** makes one book's version every book's. Each book records its own edit, so undo in any book takes back just that book's.

If the difference is deliberate (a retcon you're keeping), dismiss the finding. Dismissing it in one book dismisses it in all of them.

## Bringing in what the series has

Inside a book, each Lorebook list ends with **From the series**: what the series shares that this book doesn't have yet. **Add** brings one in where the book before left it. Lorebook › **Series** shows the whole Canon from inside the book.

The @ picker offers series characters and places this book doesn't have yet, marked **from the series**; choosing one brings it in. Proposals notice when your prose names someone from the series who isn't in this book, and when this book already has its own Eleanor that should be the series' one.

## Promises across books

A thread can run through several books: a question Book 1 asks and Book 3 answers. Carried into the next book, it is the same thread, and each book's scenes say what it does there. Its sheet says **Carried from Book 1** or **Continues in Book 2**, the tapestry runs it in from the edge and out to the next book, and **Across the series** on its sheet folds out what each book does with it.

A twist can be clued in one book and revealed in another. Its checks know the other books: clues in Book 1 prepare a reveal in Book 3, a reveal in a later book answers a misdirection, and a clue after an earlier book's reveal is a reminder, not a clue. Three checks look across the books themselves: a thread **left open** at the end of a book that no later book picks up (**Bring into Book 2** carries it on), a thread that **opens again** in a later book, and threads across books that cross rather than nest.

A setup can pay off in another book: the gun on the wall in Book 1 that fires in Book 3. On a book's **Setups**, link a scene here to a scene in another book; both books show it, and so does the tapestry.

The series' **Promises** section draws every thread and twist the books share, a column per book.

## What the reader brings with them

In Book 2, **What the reader knows** starts with **Coming in**: what the reader learned, still believes and alone knows from the books before. A belief an earlier book planted is struck through where this book's reveal overturns it.

**The story so far** on the series page is a card per book, a reminder before you write the next: its summary, where the characters end up and what changed for them, what the reader learns, believes and alone knows, and what is still open as it ends.

## Research the books share

A research entry, an image or a diagram (a map of the island, say) can be **shared with the series**: every book has it, books added later too, and an edit in any book is an edit in all of them. Each book holds its own copy, so a book exported or restored on its own is still whole. Keep one book's copy apart, or stop sharing, and every book keeps what it has. **Shared research** on the series page shows whether the copies agree, and puts them back in step when they do not.

## What the Assistant knows

In Studio mode, the Assistant working on a book knows what its characters and places were like in the books **before** it, never after. A later book is your plan for what hasn't happened yet. In an interview, a character remembers who they were in the earlier books as their past. Working on a scene, it knows the threads the earlier books left open, the twists they planted and the truth behind them, and what the reader comes in knowing.

## What undo covers

Bringing something into a book, taking it out, and making a value every book's are undone in that book. A setup across books is undone in the book you made it in. An edit to shared research is undone in the book you made it in, and the other books follow. The series' own shape (its name, its order, which things are shared) isn't undone, because undo belongs to one book.
